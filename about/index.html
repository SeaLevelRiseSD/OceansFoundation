About

Why this site was created

Sea level rise is one of the most consequential challenges facing San Diego. We built this website as a resource to make the relevant science more accessible, highlight local impacts, and encourage residents to take action

Our mission

Turning science into civic action

Sea Level Rise San Diego is an independent educational resource dedicated to helping San Diego residents, community leaders, and elected officials understand how rising seas are already impacting our coastline — and what we can do about it.

We believe that informed communities make better decisions. Our goal is to bridge the gap between peer-reviewed climate science and the important conversations happening in neighborhood meetings, city halls, and living rooms across San Diego County.

We are not affiliated with any government agency, political party, or commercial interest. We follow the science, cite our sources, and update content as new research emerges.

Science-first

Every claim on this site is grounded in peer-reviewed research or data from authoritative institutions like NOAA, Scripps, and the IPCC.

Local focus

Global data matters, but San Diegans need to understand what it means for La Jolla, Mission Bay, and the Silver Strand.

Nonpartisan

Climate change is a scientific reality, not a political position. We present the evidence clearly and let readers draw their own conclusions about policy.

Action-oriented

Awareness without action isn't enough. We work to provide San Diegans with information they need to help protect our neighborhoods and infrastructure, and safely plan for future development.

Data sources

Where our information comes from

We rely exclusively on peer-reviewed research and data from established scientific and governmental institutions.

Tide gauge data

NOAA Sea Level Trends

The definitive long-term tide gauge record for the United States, including the La Jolla gauge that has measured San Diego sea levels since 1924.

Scripps Institution of Oceanography

UC San Diego's world-leading ocean research institution, measuring sea level and ocean conditions here for over a century.

Visit source ↗ Global projections

IPCC Sixth Assessment Report

The most comprehensive synthesis of global climate science, including sea level rise projections through 2100 under multiple scenarios.

Visit source ↗ State projections

California Ocean Protection Council

California's primary state body for ocean policy and the source of official projections used in coastal planning.

Visit source ↗ Local policy

City of San Diego Climate Action Plan

San Diego's roadmap for reducing emissions and adapting to climate change along the city's 17 miles of coastline.

Visit source ↗ Erosion & hazards

USGS Coastal Change Hazards Portal

Coastal erosion, storm impact, and sea level rise vulnerability data for Southern California beaches.

Visit source ↗ Satellite data

NASA Sea Level Change Portal

Satellite-based sea level measurements and projections that complement the long-running tide gauge record.

Visit source ↗ Federal scenarios

NOAA Interagency Sea Level Rise Scenarios

The definitive federal sea level rise scenario report used for infrastructure and coastal planning.
